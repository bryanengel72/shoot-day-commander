# Communication Templates

## The no-send rule

Every message here is drafted and handed over. Nothing in this skill sends employee-facing
mail, no matter how the request is phrased. These messages go to dozens of people inside a
client's company, and a mistake lands on the photographer's relationship with that client. Draft
it, show it, let them send it. `Gmail:create_draft` can stage it in their own drafts folder if
they want it there.

Use `[CONFIRM: ...]` for anything not actually known. Never invent a room number, a contact
name, or a slot.

## 1. Coordinator brief

To the client-side contact, once the schedule exists. This is the message that prevents most
day-of confusion.

> Subject: [Company] headshot day, logistics and what we need from you
>
> Hi [Coordinator],
>
> Here's everything for [date]. I'll arrive at [arrival time] to set up and the first person is
> at [first slot]. We should wrap around [wrap time].
>
> What I need on your end:
> - Room access from [arrival time]. I need roughly a 10x12 clear area and a standard outlet.
> - A waiting spot right next to the room, so the next person is a few steps away. This one
>   makes a bigger difference to the day than it sounds like.
> - One person owning day-of flow, checking people in and keeping things moving. Doesn't have to
>   be you.
> - If the room runs warm, worth setting the thermostat a touch cooler. Studio lights add heat
>   over a full day.
>
> What I handle: all equipment and lighting, posing and expression direction for every person,
> shooting, culling, retouching and delivery. Nobody needs to be briefed on how to pose, that's
> my job.
>
> Full schedule attached. Anyone who needs extra time or accommodation, just flag them and I'll
> build in room.
>
> [Sender Name]

## 2. Employee prep email

Roughly a week out. Sent by the client to their team, so it is written for them to forward or
adapt.

The one rule that matters most: **give each person their specific slot.** "You're scheduled
Tuesday at 10:15" is actionable. "Sometime Tuesday morning" produces a hallway full of people
and a schedule that collapses by mid-morning.

> Subject: Your headshot time on [date]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> We're doing team headshots on [date] and your slot is **[time]** in [room].
>
> It takes about [X] minutes. Genuinely that short, so no need to block out your morning.
>
> A few things that help:
> - **Wear solid colors.** Busy patterns, thin stripes, and logos all fight the camera. Business
>   attire that fits how your industry actually dresses.
> - **Bring a backup top.** Sometimes a color reads differently under studio lighting than it
>   does in the mirror.
> - **Haircut 3 to 5 days ahead**, not the day before. Fresh cuts photograph better after they
>   settle.
> - **Check glasses for smudges** and make sure necklaces hang straight.
> - **Don't worry about blemishes.** Those get handled in retouching.
>
> See you [date].

## 3. The 48-hour reminder

Short on purpose. Time, room, and the duration line.

> Subject: Headshot reminder, [day] at [time]
>
> Quick reminder: your headshot is [day] at **[time]** in [room]. It only takes about [X]
> minutes.
>
> Solid colors photograph best, and bring a backup top if you want options.

The duration line is doing real work in that message. A meaningful share of last-minute
cancellations come from people who are nervous about being photographed and quietly avoid it.
Naming how short it is reduces that.

## 4. Selection nudges (gallery-after model only)

Three different situations, three different messages. Sending "have you picked yet" to someone
who never opened the gallery just annoys them.

**Never opened:**
> Subject: Your headshot gallery
>
> Hi [Name], your headshots from [date] are ready here: [link]. Pick your favorite and I'll get
> it retouched and over to you.

**Opened, no pick:**
> Subject: Which one?
>
> Hi [Name], saw you had a look at your gallery from [date]. Whenever you land on one, just flag
> it and I'll get it retouched. Happy to give you my pick if you want a tiebreaker.

**Coordinator summary**, usually the one that actually moves the needle:
> Subject: [Company] headshots, still waiting on [N] selections
>
> Hi [Coordinator],
>
> Almost done with [Company]. Still waiting on picks from:
>
> [list]
>
> Once those land I can finish retouching and deliver the full set. A nudge from you tends to
> land better than another email from me.
>
> [Sender Name]
